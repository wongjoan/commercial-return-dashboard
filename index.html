How this workflow works

Not a label — a chain of evidence: performance gap → the KPI causing it → possible cause (4-quarter pattern, peer comparison, leading indicators, and for HR/Executive review the employee's own survey signals) → a suggested improvement action → manager review (an agreed action plan and review date) → escalation if the gap persists.

Escalation path: Manager → Senior Manager → Executive / HR review. A manager can record a plan, escalate or resolve while the case is with them; once escalated, only the senior manager (then the Director/HR) can act. The suggested stage comes from how many consecutive quarters the score has been below 75% — every stage move is a manual, audit-logged action.

Confidentiality: individual pulse/onboarding survey answers and pay-related flags are shown only at Executive/HR review level. Managers and senior managers see team-level survey themes only, and only for groups of 5 or more. Employees never see case stages or notes; their own page shows an "improvement focus" instead.

Persistence: cases are stored server-side (no more CSV import); the CSV download remains for hand-off and offline review.

What's real vs. illustrative in this build

One app, four hierarchy levels: Director, Senior Manager, Manager and Employee each sign in and see only what their level allows — enforced by the server, not by hiding things in the page. Everyone who is scored also has "My performance" (their own scorecard, with no pay information).

Anonymised: names are fictional. Role, department, pay structure and pay amounts follow the supplied commercial roster; each person is linked to a synthetic HR record (tenure, location, pulse/onboarding surveys) from the supplied dummy HR dataset.

Dummy, deterministic: KPI targets, actuals and 4-quarter history, expense claims, statutory/benefit costs and individual attributed NGP. The KPI definitions in section 3 are Glocomp's real formulas.

Illustrative, pending real deal-level financials: company revenue/NGP/EBITDA/collections, department attributed NGP, projects and the attribution-governance mechanics in section 6.

1. One cascade, four checkpoints

Every deal and every period is tracked through the same four checkpoints, so "performance" always means the same thing company-wide:

Revenue booked → less direct cost of sale → Net Gross Profit (NGP) → less allocated opex → EBITDA → Collections (cash actually received)

NGP is used for attribution below the company level because it is directly traceable to a deal. EBITDA already has shared overhead baked in, so it is only meaningful once things are rolled up to department or company level. Collections is tracked as its own lens — profitable revenue that isn't collected isn't a return yet.

2. Role-based KPI templates and weightage

Each role has a KPI template: a financial vs. non-financial split, and KPI weights inside each group (each group sums to 100%). Templates are stored configuration, not code — see the KPI Weightage tab. The Director can change any role, Senior Managers the roles in their departments; a Manager can agree an individual split (within ±15 points of the template) and individual targets with a team member. Employees see their weightage but cannot change it. Every change needs a reason, can be previewed, and is audit-logged.

KPI points = group weight × KPI weight in group × min(actual ÷ target, 100%) Overall = financial points + non-financial points e.g. Financial 60% × Revenue 50% = 30 pts possible; 91.6% of target → 27.5 pts

Default splits (illustrative starting points to agree with function heads): Sales 60/40 · Product Management 35/65 · Delivery 30/70 · Consulting 20/80 · Sales Support 10/90. Click any score in the app to see the full calculation.

4. Cost coverage ratio

Answers "is this contribution worth what we're paying for it?" — at whichever level you're looking at.

Cost Coverage Ratio = Commercial Contribution ÷ Total Employment Cost (Total Employment Cost = salary + commission/incentive paid + benefits + training + entertainment)

Company level uses EBITDA as the contribution figure (post-overhead, the real bottom line). Department and individual level use attributed NGP, since that is what the attribution matrix can fairly split. Thresholds: below 1.0× for two consecutive periods is flagged for review; 1.0–1.5× is acceptable; above 1.5× is healthy.

5. Shared-contribution attribution (no double counting)

Every deal or project's NGP is treated as a fixed pool worth exactly 100%. Before any deal closes, function heads agree a standard attribution template per deal type (new logo, renewal/upsell, project delivery, etc.) — e.g. a standard new-logo deal splits Sales 55% / Sales Support 30% / Product Management 10% / Consulting 5%. The template is applied automatically by deal type at close, not negotiated per deal, which is what prevents both double counting (the pool never exceeds 100%) and gaming (nobody argues their percentage after the fact). Each function's dashboard only ever shows its attributed share.

6. Attribution governance

The templates above aren't a static config file — they're versioned. A change is forward-dated only: it registers a new version that governs deals closing from a future quarter, and it can never rewrite the split a deal already closed under (Rulebook §8.3). Any change that would cut a function's share below what it currently gets needs sign-off before it can register — cutting someone's share isn't a quiet save. Registering a new version is a Director setting — visible to every management level, editable only by the Director (the "Governance edit" control next to the tabs).

7. Bonus vs. commission/incentive employees

The dashboard tags every employee's pay structure and reads them differently: bonus-based employees are measured purely on weighted KPI achievement, which drives a bonus multiplier; commission/incentive-based employees are paid on a formula against commission-eligible revenue/NGP, but their KPI achievement is still tracked for visibility and coaching, it just isn't what determines their pay.

8. What gets auto-flagged

  • Performance gap — overall score below 70%; or 70–80% with a heavily weighted KPI under 85% of target; or a score that has declined four quarters running (HR Intervention tab, for the employee's management chain)
  • Pay equity — base salary more than 10% below the role median (Director/HR only)
  • Income volatility — commission staff with over 35% of OTE in variable pay (Director/HR only)
  • Cost coverage below 1.0× and project margin/collections issues (Company and Projects tabs, illustrative model)

9. HR intervention workflow

Every case is a chain of evidence, not a label: performance gap → affected KPI → possible cause/context → suggested improvement action → manager review → escalation if required. Causes come from the 4-quarter pattern, a peer comparison (team-wide vs. individual), leading indicators that are also below target and — at Executive/HR review only — the person's own survey signals.

Escalation: Manager (records an action plan and review date, can escalate or resolve) → Senior Manager (can escalate, return to manager or resolve) → Executive/HR review (resolved · continue structured support · redeploy · end employment). The suggested stage comes from consecutive quarters below 75%; every move is manual and audit-logged. Cases are stored server-side; the CSV download is for hand-off.
