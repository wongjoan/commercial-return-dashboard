"Detailed (for HR)" shows the same people with the full pay-band graphic and an editable stage/owner/notes case tracker. Either view can open the full detailed report, or export/import the same CSV.

Escalation path, not a one-shot label: a case moves Flagged → department manager → senior manager → executive review, where the decision is to continue the intervention or end employment. Real, from the data provided: pay-equity outliers (base salary materially below the role median) and income-volatility exposure (commission staff heavily dependent on variable pay) — both computed directly from the real compensation columns.

Illustrative, pending real deal-level financials: revenue, NGP, EBITDA, collections, the department cost/coverage figures and margin waterfall on the Company, Departments and Projects tabs, the attribution-governance mechanics in section 6, and the attributed deal pool on the separate employee dashboard — the department headcounts driving them already match the real roster, but no deal-level revenue or cost data was provided to replace the financial model itself.

1. One cascade, four checkpoints

Every deal and every period is tracked through the same four checkpoints, so "performance" always means the same thing company-wide:

Revenue booked → less direct cost of sale → Net Gross Profit (NGP) → less allocated opex → EBITDA → Collections (cash actually received)

NGP is used for attribution below the company level because it is directly traceable to a deal. EBITDA already has shared overhead baked in, so it is only meaningful once things are rolled up to department or company level. Collections is tracked as its own lens — profitable revenue that isn't collected isn't a return yet.

2. Role-based KPI templates

Each role has an approved KPI template — a fixed set of weighted measures agreed with the function head. Actuals are scored against target per measure, then combined into one weighted achievement percentage per person, per period. This is what "KPI achievement" means everywhere in this dashboard. The mix behind that one number is different per function — only Sales is scored mostly on revenue; functions that don't carry a quota are weighted mostly on non-financial measures instead:

RoleBasisMeasures & weightsWhy
SalesMostly financialRevenue 40% · NGP 30% · Collections 20% · Non-financial 10%Quota-carrying and closest to the money
Product ManagementMixedAttributed NGP 35% · Deal-support win rate 25% · Roadmap delivery on time 20% · Sales-enablement response SLA 20%Influences deals without owning them
ConsultingMostly non-financialScoping accuracy 30% · Utilisation rate 25% · Delivery quality score 25% · On-time scoping turnaround 20%No sales quota — measured on whether estimates and delivery hold up
Sales SupportMostly non-financialTurnaround SLA 30% · Order/contract accuracy 30% · Renewal admin completion 20% · Internal CSAT 20%Operational backbone — speed and accuracy, not revenue they don't generate
DeliveryMixedProject margin 30% · On-time delivery rate 25% · Client satisfaction/quality score 25% · Collections/DSO influence 20%Owns post-close execution — margin and collections matter, but so does quality and timeliness

All weights are illustrative — the real numbers would be agreed with each function head before rollout, same as the deal-type attribution templates in section 6.

4. Cost coverage ratio

Answers "is this contribution worth what we're paying for it?" — at whichever level you're looking at.

Cost Coverage Ratio = Commercial Contribution ÷ Total Employment Cost (Total Employment Cost = salary + commission/incentive paid + benefits + training + entertainment)

Company level uses EBITDA as the contribution figure (post-overhead, the real bottom line). Department and individual level use attributed NGP, since that is what the attribution matrix can fairly split. Thresholds: below 1.0× for two consecutive periods is flagged for review; 1.0–1.5× is acceptable; above 1.5× is healthy.

5. Shared-contribution attribution (no double counting)

Every deal or project's NGP is treated as a fixed pool worth exactly 100%. Before any deal closes, function heads agree a standard attribution template per deal type (new logo, renewal/upsell, project delivery, etc.) — e.g. a standard new-logo deal splits Sales 55% / Sales Support 30% / Product Management 10% / Consulting 5%. The template is applied automatically by deal type at close, not negotiated per deal, which is what prevents both double counting (the pool never exceeds 100%) and gaming (nobody argues their percentage after the fact). Each function's dashboard only ever shows its attributed share.

6. Attribution governance

The templates above aren't a static config file — they're versioned. A change is forward-dated only: it registers a new version that governs deals closing from a future quarter, and it can never rewrite the split a deal already closed under (Rulebook §8.3). Any change that would cut a function's share below what it currently gets needs sign-off before it can register — cutting someone's share isn't a quiet save. Registering a new version is an employer/admin setting — visible to everyone, editable only in "Employer" view (see the toggle top-right, and the "Governance edit" control next to the tabs).

7. Bonus vs. commission/incentive employees

The dashboard tags every employee's pay structure and reads them differently: bonus-based employees are measured purely on weighted KPI achievement, which drives a bonus multiplier; commission/incentive-based employees are paid on a formula against commission-eligible revenue/NGP, but their KPI achievement is still tracked for visibility and coaching, it just isn't what determines their pay.

8. What gets auto-flagged

  • Cost coverage ratio below 1.0× for two consecutive periods (illustrative model)
  • KPI achievement below 70% (individual) for two consecutive periods (illustrative model, pending section 3 actuals)
  • Collections lag beyond the 60-day DSO threshold (illustrative model)
  • Project margin below target with collections also lagging (illustrative model)
  • Pay equity — base salary more than 10% below the role median (real data, live on the Individuals and HR Intervention tabs)
  • Income volatility — commission staff with over 35% of OTE in variable pay (real data, live on the Individuals and HR Intervention tabs)
  • Performance — simulated KPI achievement below 70% (same figure as section 10; live on the HR Intervention tab only, feeding the management approval cards there)

9. HR intervention workflow

The HR Intervention tab is the one part of this dashboard built directly from the client's ask for "HR intervention" features. It has two views of the same flagged list, switchable at the top of the tab:

  • Simple (for managers) — each flagged employee is a card with plain-language reasons ("paid about 12% less than others in the same role," not a percentile chart), a recommended next step (which specific measure to address, drawn from the financial and non-financial breakdown), and the case's current escalation stage — never just a good/bad label.
  • Detailed (for HR) — the same people, with the full pay-band graphic (P25/median/P75), the underlying flag detail, and an editable escalation Stage, Owner and Notes field.

Either view can open a full detailed report per person (a modal, in place of a separate window) breaking the case down into its financial component, its named non-financial measure with actual vs. target, pay context where relevant, and a full list of recommendations.

Escalation path: Flagged → with department manager → with senior manager → executive review, where the final call is to continue the intervention or end employment. The starting stage is suggested from a simulated "quarters flagged" count (matching the real-world idea that a one-off dip stays with the department manager, but something recurring for 3-4 quarters should reach senior management or executives) — every move up or down the path, and the final continue/terminate/resolved decision, is a manual action taken from the card or the report.

Three checks feed the flagged list:

  • Pay equity check (real data) — for any role with more than one person, flags anyone whose base salary sits over 10% below that role's median. Ten of 64 employees currently clear this line.
  • Income volatility check (real data) — for commission-paid staff, flags anyone whose variable pay exceeds 35% of their OTE, since a bad quarter hits their take-home hard. Ten of 24 commission-paid employees currently clear this line.
  • Performance check (simulated — see section 10) — flags anyone whose simulated KPI achievement sits below 70%, and is what drives the financial/non-financial recommendation in the detailed report; it is not a real measurement yet, and the report says so.

Deliberately not included in this tab: no coaching flag or termination-risk score beyond the disclosed simulated KPI check above is attached to any named employee, because no real tenure or engagement data was supplied. See the callout at the top of this tab for what a fuller build would need. The case tracker itself is in-memory for this session — CSV export/import (including each person's escalation stage, final decision, and estimated quarters flagged) is how work carries over between sessions or gets handed to a colleague.

10. Simulated KPI achievement (Individuals, Departments, employee dashboard)

The "KPI achievement" badges on the Individuals and Departments tabs, and on the separate employee dashboard, turn the section 3 formulas into a number, so they demonstrate the calculation instead of staying purely descriptive. Because no real actuals for those formulas were in the dataset, the figures are a deterministic simulation — the same seed always produces the same number for a given person, so results don't shuffle on every reload, but they are not a measurement of anyone's real performance.

KPI Achievement (sim.) = financial-share × simulated financial score + (1 − financial-share) × simulated score against the section-3 formula

The financial share per department matches the illustrative weights in section 2 (e.g. Sales 90% financial, Consulting 0%, Delivery 30%). Thresholds mirror the rest of the dashboard: below 70% is critical, 70–90% is a watch item, above 90% is healthy.

Every place this figure appears is labeled "(sim.)" or "(simulated)" for exactly this reason — wiring in the real order counts, SLA logs, survey ratings and timesheets behind section 3 would let this become a real, scored number without changing the formula or the UI around it.
